# You're probably not going to finish reading this.

Why? Because you've been conditioned by AI to no longer read large blocks of text.

You're losing basic skills.

So what's the solution? Stop using AI?

No, I'm not saying that at all. But every tool comes with its cons, and we need a better way to use AI without sacrificing critical basic skills.

This is one important reason to use Ergo. But here's a bigger reason:



# AI is a black box.

You put text in a prompt, pull the lever on the magic slot machine, and hope you win. It's not really clear what it's thinking, why it decided to do something, or how you can control it.

It doesn't understand your big-picture.

And as long as it stays ignorant, you're left with results that are just its best guess. Sometimes good, but often not quite right. Sometimes, hilariously wrong.

## What if you could give it a better idea of what you're thinking?

With enough context on your project and what you want done, your results can basically be _perfect every time_.

This is one of the primary pain-points Ergo attempts to solve.

I personally dealt with the same issues you have. I understand the pain of going back and forth with an AI, often with mixed results. So I built this system, and spent months refining it. What you now have access to is the result of my efforts.

Here's how the app works at a high level:

- **You write out a big todo list.** Whatever work needs to be done, write it down - specify all the ins and outs. Nest subtasks, format text, add pictures. Whatever layout works best for you.
- **You tell the AI what to work on.** Assign specific tasks one-by-one, schedule tasks, or run in batches. It's flexible enough to handle lots of different workflows, but structured enough to keep you from getting lost.
- **The AI looks at your tasks in context.** The more data you put in your todo list, the more it understands how _you_ want it to work, not how _it thinks_ you want it to work. This makes it more of a collaborator alongside you, and increases its accuracy.
